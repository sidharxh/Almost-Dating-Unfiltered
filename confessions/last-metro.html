The Last Metro Was Not a Romantic Deadline

Confession #004 · Fictional story · For adults 18+

Posted by u/LastTrainFirstDate · Dating Stories

We matched on Hinge during a week when I was honestly too tired to date.

Work was exhausting, my savings were lower than I'd planned, and my social battery was basically a dying phone at 2%.

But her profile made me laugh, so we arranged a Friday evening date at a café near a busy market.

I wore my favourite shirt. She arrived in a simple black dress and sneakers, which I found ridiculously attractive.

We talked about everything: terrible bosses, family expectations, comfort movies, and the strange confidence people develop when texting from the safety of their bedroom.

Three hours passed without either of us noticing.

Then I checked the time.

My metro connection was getting close.

I hesitated because I didn't want to seem like I was trying to escape. She noticed and asked whether I needed to leave.

I admitted I did.

She smiled and said she needed to get home too.

That was surprisingly reassuring. Neither of us had to pretend that romance meant abandoning our responsibilities.

Outside the station, we paused.

She asked, "Can I hug you?"

I said yes.

We hugged, and I remember thinking that I wanted to see her again more than I wanted to extend that particular evening.

On the train, I received a message.

"Reached home safely?"

I replied that I was still on my way and asked her the same question.

She told me she was home, sent me a picture of her cat looking furious, and we exchanged messages until I reached my own place.

Our second date was the following Saturday.

We planned it earlier in the day, chose a place that fit both our budgets, and didn't have to worry about catching the last train.

What I learned: Romantic tension doesn't require reckless decisions. Sort out your transport, meet somewhere public, and don't treat leaving on time as evidence that someone isn't interested. A person who likes you should care about your comfort and safety, too.
