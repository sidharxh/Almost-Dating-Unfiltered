My Parents Were Asleep in the Next Room

Jaipur | M, 27 · 18+

I still live with my parents. Decent job, decent salary, zero chance of privacy unless the entire household suddenly develops an interest in sleeping early.

I matched with a woman on Hinge who lived nearby. Our first date was at a café, and we spent three hours discussing everything from bad bosses to why Indian parents treat marriage like a project with a deadline.

We met again the following weekend.

She came over to return a book I'd lent her. My parents were home, so we sat in the living room and behaved like two candidates in a family-approved interview.

My mother offered her tea. My father asked about her job. I wanted the floor to open up and swallow me.

After dinner, my parents went to their room.

She looked at me and whispered, “Your family is sweet.”

“You say that because you haven't seen my childhood report cards.”

She laughed, then leaned closer.

We kissed. Slowly at first, then with the kind of nervous excitement that makes you forget every clever thing you've ever said. We kept pausing whenever we heard a sound in the hallway, trying not to laugh.

At one point, she rested her forehead against mine and whispered, “We're literally behaving like teenagers.”

“We're adults with no independent housing,” I replied. “It's different.”

She nearly laughed loud enough to summon my mother.

Before she left, she squeezed my hand and said she wanted to see me again.

Not secretly. Not just when the house was empty.

That was the moment the whole thing became more than chemistry.
