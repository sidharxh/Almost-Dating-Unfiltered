The Rain Ruined Our Plans. Then Things Got Interesting.

Confession #002 · Fictional story · For adults 18+

Posted by u/MonsoonMeetCute · Dating Stories

I was 28 when I matched with someone who seemed almost suspiciously normal.

No games. No disappearing for three days. No mysterious "I'm bad at texting" speech followed by twenty Instagram stories.

We planned coffee after work on Friday. I'd even ironed my shirt, which should tell you how seriously I was taking this.

Then it rained.

Not romantic-drizzle rain. Proper Indian monsoon rain that makes you question every decision you've made since waking up.

I messaged her that we could reschedule.

She replied with a picture of her completely soaked sneakers.

"Plan B? Pakoras and chai?"

Twenty minutes later, we were sitting in a modest little café near a bus stop, sharing a plate of pakoras.

She told me she was saving money for a solo trip. I told her I'd been trying to save for a better laptop while helping with expenses at home.

There was something unexpectedly attractive about talking to someone who understood that life involved EMIs, family responsibilities, and occasionally choosing between ordering food and cooking whatever was already in the fridge.

We talked for two hours.

At one point, she looked directly at me and said, "You know, you don't have to agree with everything I say."

I asked what she meant.

She said she'd noticed I kept adjusting my opinions to match hers. Apparently, I'd agreed with her about three completely contradictory things.

I was embarrassed, but she wasn't making fun of me. She wanted to know what I actually thought.

That conversation changed the entire evening.

I stopped trying to be the perfect date. I told her which movies I hated, why I wanted to change jobs, and how I sometimes worried that I wasn't doing enough with my life.

She disagreed with me about half of it.

It was wonderful.

When we finally left, she asked if she could hug me. I said yes, and we stood there for a few seconds longer than a casual goodbye required.

On the way home, she messaged: "Next time, I'm choosing the snacks."

We met again the following weekend.

What I learned: Confidence isn't pretending to have everything figured out. Sometimes the most attractive thing you can do is stop performing and let someone meet the actual person behind your profile.
