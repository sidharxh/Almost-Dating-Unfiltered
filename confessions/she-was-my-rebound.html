She Was Supposed to Be My Rebound

Bengaluru | M, 29

My ex broke up with me after four years together. I spent the next month pretending I was fine, going to work, hitting the gym, and checking her profile at 1 AM like an unpaid private investigator.

Then I downloaded Hinge.

I matched with a woman who had written, “I will judge your playlist, not your salary.”

We met at a tiny café. She wore a black kurta, had an unfairly good smile, and roasted my music taste within seven minutes.

I liked her immediately, which was inconvenient because I had a whole tragic-man routine planned.

Our dates became a habit. Sunday breakfast, evening walks, sharing headphones on the metro, sending each other stupid reels during work.

One night, we were sitting on her balcony. She was wearing my jacket, and I was trying very hard not to stare at her mouth every time she spoke.

She noticed.

“Are you going to kiss me or keep pretending you're thinking about the weather?”

I kissed her.

She kissed me back, then pulled away just enough to look at me.

“Are you actually over your ex?”

I hated that question because it was fair.

I told her the truth: I wasn't completely over the past, but I didn't want to use her to escape it.

She said she wasn't interested in being someone's emotional waiting room.

We slowed things down. I stopped checking my ex's profile. I started showing up for this woman as herself, not as a cure.

Three months later, we're still dating.

She still judges my playlist.

She was right about the music, too.
