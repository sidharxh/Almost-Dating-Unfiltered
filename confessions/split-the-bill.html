We Split the Bill, Then Forgot to Leave

Confession #001 · Fictional story · For adults 18+

Posted by u/ChaiAndBadDecisions · Dating & Relationships

I'm 26, she's 25, and I almost cancelled our first date because I had ₹1,200 left in my fun budget until payday.

We matched on Hinge after she commented on my prompt about judging people by their music taste. Three days of memes and voice notes later, she suggested chai and momos near the metro station.

Honestly, I was relieved. No expensive restaurant, no pretending I knew anything about wine.

I reached fifteen minutes early and spent those fifteen minutes checking my hair in my phone camera. When she arrived, she was wearing jeans, a simple top, and the kind of smile that immediately made me forget the clever opening line I'd rehearsed.

We ordered two teas and one plate of momos.

The conversation started awkwardly. We discussed work, traffic, and how both of us had lied about enjoying morning workouts on our dating profiles. Then she told me about the time she accidentally sent a complaint about her manager directly to her manager.

I laughed so hard that tea came out of my nose.

When the bill arrived, I reached for it. She looked at me and said, "Let's split it. I want a second date, not a financial audit."

That was the moment I realised I genuinely liked her.

Afterward, we walked through the market. We bought absolutely nothing, judged terrible T-shirt designs, and stopped to feed a street dog. She stole the last bite of my roasted corn and then accused me of being possessive when I protested.

Eventually, we reached the metro entrance.

Neither of us seemed particularly interested in saying goodbye.

I asked if I could hold her hand. She smiled and slipped hers into mine.

It was such a small thing, but my entire brain stopped functioning.

We didn't kiss that night. We didn't need to manufacture some dramatic ending. Before boarding her train, she said, "I had fun. Your movie opinions are still alarming, though."

I spent the entire ride home smiling at my phone like an idiot.

Edit: We're dating now. Our second date was a ₹300 street-food adventure. She still steals my food.

What I learned: A hot date isn't necessarily an expensive date. It's one where both people feel attractive, comfortable, and wanted without trying to impress each other financially.
